# Areas for improvement

## 1. The homepage positioning is too broad

“Technologist,” “leadership,” “insights,” and “real-world solutions” are directionally true, but they are generic. Lots of people can claim that. Your actual edge is much sharper.

Your homepage should immediately answer:

* What kind of leader are you?
* What kinds of problems do you solve?
* What proof do you have?
* Why should someone keep reading?

A stronger hero direction:

> **Engineering leader building AI-enabled systems that make business operations faster, clearer, and more scalable.**
> I lead teams that modernize business tooling, streamline complex operational workflows, and turn ambiguous cross-functional problems into durable systems.

That is much closer to who you are now.

## 2. The site probably under-represents Team GSD

This is the biggest content gap.

Since the site was generated, your Team GSD story has become central:

* AI-first tiger team
* People First, AI Empowered
* workflow automation
* LLM-enabled process improvement
* Customer Support and Finance as early targets
* Easy Wins / Initiatives / Reworks prioritization model
* n8n, Cursor, browser-based AI, ChatGPT Enterprise, NotebookLM
* operational transformation without replacing people

That needs to be a first-class section, not a footnote.

Suggested section:

> **AI Transformation & Team GSD**
> How we identify manual work, redesign workflows, and use automation/AI to increase capacity without losing the human judgment that makes teams effective.

## 3. The site likely undersells your measurable impact

Your strongest professional proof points need to be easy to scan:

* 300% increase in supported BSE project throughput
* <1% MR rollback rate after release/test planning improvements
* NetSuite Sales Order transaction reduction from 7 to 4
* 95% of Sales Orders shifted to NetSuite out-of-box invoicing
* Asana asynchronous project status adopted across five divisions
* BSE domain documentation improving onboarding and cross-team understanding
* SO/IF lifecycle visibility dashboard
* Go-to-Ruby / Systems v2 architecture reducing stack fragmentation

Those should not be buried in prose. They should be visual proof blocks.

Example:

| Impact Area                     |                       Result |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------: |
| Project throughput              |               ~300% increase |
| Release reliability             |            <1% rollback rate |
| NetSuite transaction complexity |                43% reduction |
| Invoice ownership migration     | 95% moved to out-of-box flow |
| Async project visibility        |   Adopted across 5 divisions |

This is the kind of proof that turns a portfolio into an executive credibility tool.

## 4. The site should move from “portfolio” to “case study system”

Right now, the site likely behaves like a broad personal portfolio. That is fine for early career. But your current work deserves deeper case studies.

You need pages like:

1. **Scaling BSE Project Throughput by 300%**
2. **Building Asynchronous Information Flow with Asana**
3. **Modernizing Business Systems Architecture**
4. **Team GSD: AI-Enabled Workflow Transformation**
5. **Project Lead Framework: Scaling Leadership Through Delegation**
6. **Release & Test Plan Process: Improving Reliability Without Slowing Delivery**

Each case study should follow the same structure:

* Problem
* Context
* Constraints
* What I changed
* Systems/processes introduced
* Measurable impact
* Lessons learned
* Reusable framework

That structure matches your actual working style and makes the site feel senior.

## 5. The UX likely needs tightening on mobile and motion

A LinkedIn comment from the launch specifically said the mobile scrolling transitions were “a little funky,” though the overall site was impressive. ([LinkedIn][2]) That is a very believable issue for a vibe-coded portfolio.

For a senior leadership site, motion should feel premium, not distracting.

Fixes:

* reduce scroll-jacking or heavy reveal animations
* prefer subtle opacity/translate transitions
* respect `prefers-reduced-motion`
* audit mobile spacing and sticky nav behavior
* make cards and sections readable without animation dependency
* test on iPhone Safari, Chrome Android, and desktop

## 6. The information architecture probably needs a stronger hierarchy

A strong 2026 version should not just have “About / Projects / Blog / Contact.”

I’d restructure around your professional narrative:

1. **Home**
2. **About**
3. **Leadership Portfolio**
4. **AI Transformation**
5. **Case Studies**
6. **Writing**
7. **Now / Operating Principles**
8. **Contact**

Optional but strong:

* `/uses` — tools, workflows, AI stack
* `/speaking` — future-facing, even if lightly populated
* `/resume` — clean downloadable resume
* `/now` — current focus: fatherhood, Team GSD, AI transformation, leadership growth

## 7. The site needs a clearer audience decision

Right now, the audience may be too broad: recruiters, coworkers, friends, leaders, technologists, blog readers.

You should choose primary and secondary audiences.

Primary audience:

> Senior engineering/product/business leaders who want to understand your leadership philosophy, systems thinking, and ability to drive cross-functional transformation.

Secondary audience:

> Engineers, managers, and operators looking for practical frameworks around async work, AI-enabled operations, and team scaling.

That decision should drive the whole redesign.

## 8. The site should better reflect your current career arc

Since the original site, your positioning has changed:

* You are now a Software Engineering Manager, not just someone aiming toward it.
* You are leading multiple teams / responsibility areas.
* You are building Team GSD.
* You are working at the intersection of engineering, business operations, and AI transformation.
* You are a new father, which has sharpened your interest in sustainable systems, routines, and operating intentionally.
* You are writing more seriously and want to preserve your own voice.

The site should feel less like “aspiring senior leader” and more like “operator with a clear executive trajectory.”

## 9. The writing should preserve your voice but remove generic AI polish

Because the original site was vibe-coded, some copy may feel a little too clean, broad, or AI-generic. That is dangerous for you because your real voice is one of your assets.

Avoid phrases like:

* “passionate about technology”
* “building teams that thrive”
* “real-world solutions”
* “leveraging innovation”
* “driving impact”

Use more grounded, Chris-style language:

* “I like taking messy systems and making them understandable.”
* “A lot of my work sits between engineering and the business teams that depend on us.”
* “The goal is not to use AI for the sake of using AI. The goal is to make work clearer, faster, and less frustrating.”
* “Good systems reduce cognitive load.”

That sounds more like you and less like a template.

# Technical refactor recommendations

Assuming this is currently a vibe-coded React/site-builder-style implementation, I would refactor toward a maintainable, content-driven architecture.

## Preferred stack

Use:

* **Next.js** or Astro
* TypeScript
* Tailwind
* MDX for writing/case studies
* Content collections for structured content
* Vercel or Netlify
* Plausible / Vercel Analytics
* OpenGraph image generation
* SEO metadata per page

My bias: **Astro** if the site is mostly content and performance matters. **Next.js** if you want more interactive components and future app-like features.

For you, I’d probably choose **Astro + React islands** unless you specifically want to keep building React-heavy components.

## Refactor priorities

1. Replace hardcoded content with structured content files.
2. Add a design system: typography, spacing, cards, buttons, layout primitives.
3. Build reusable case study templates.
4. Add SEO metadata and OpenGraph images.
5. Add analytics.
6. Improve mobile performance.
7. Audit accessibility.
8. Add test/build validation.
9. Add content preview/dev workflow.
10. Add CI/CD checks.

## Suggested content model

Create collections for:

* `caseStudies`
* `posts`
* `projects`
* `achievements`
* `principles`

Example case study fields:

```ts
title
summary
problem
role
timeline
systemsIntroduced
impactMetrics
tools
lessons
confidentialityNote
featured
```

This keeps the site maintainable and lets you add new proof points without redesigning pages.
