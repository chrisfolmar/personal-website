Yes — the **design is where the biggest opportunity is**.

The current site appears to have the bones of a polished personal portfolio, but the next version should feel more like a **premium leadership/product site**: cleaner hierarchy, stronger visual identity, more intentional storytelling, and less “AI-generated portfolio template” energy.

## My design diagnosis

The current design likely does a decent job of being modern and approachable, but it probably does **not yet visually communicate** the level you’re operating at now.

Right now, the design impression is probably close to:

> Smart technologist. Clean portfolio. AI-assisted personal site. Friendly and modern.

The target impression should be:

> Senior engineering leader. Systems thinker. Operator. Modernizes business workflows. Builds scalable teams and technical systems. Clear, credible, calm, and high-trust.

That difference matters.

---

# What the design should become

## 1. More editorial, less generic portfolio

Your site should feel less like a grid of cards and sections, and more like a strong professional publication / leadership portfolio.

Think:

* Stripe-level clarity
* Linear-level restraint
* Notion-style readability
* GitHub-profile technical credibility
* personal warmth, but not startup-template gloss

The design should make someone think:

> “This person has taste, discipline, and a clear point of view.”

Not:

> “This is a nice React portfolio.”

# Color palette

I would avoid overly colorful gradients unless they are very restrained.

A strong palette for you:

## Option A: Executive technical dark mode

* Background: near-black navy / charcoal
* Surface: slightly lighter slate
* Text: warm off-white
* Muted text: slate gray
* Accent: Fullscript-adjacent green or soft mint
* Secondary accent: muted blue

This fits your Fullscript/Ruby/AI/business systems world.

## Option B: Warm professional light mode

* Background: warm ivory / off-white
* Surface: white
* Text: deep charcoal
* Accent: deep green
* Secondary accent: muted gold or slate blue

This is more approachable and executive.

## My recommendation

Use **light mode as the default** with a very good dark mode.

Why? Because for senior leadership credibility, light mode often feels more editorial, readable, and mature. Dark mode can still be excellent, but if overdone it can drift into “developer portfolio template.”

# Typography

This is a major lever.

Use a strong pairing:

## Headings

Something sharp and modern:

* `Inter Tight`
* `Satoshi`
* `Geist`
* `Aeonik` if licensed
* `Space Grotesk` if you want a slightly more technical feel

## Body

Use something extremely readable:

* `Inter`
* `Geist Sans`
* `Source Sans 3`

Avoid anything too futuristic. Your work is technical, but your audience is leadership. You need clarity over sci-fi.

## Design rule

Big headings. Short paragraphs. Strong rhythm.

Your homepage hero should not be a wall of copy. It should hit hard in 2–3 lines.

# Motion and interaction

This is an important one.

Given the prior mobile feedback around funky scroll transitions, I would simplify motion heavily.

Use:

* subtle fade-ins
* small hover lifts
* section reveal, only once
* animated system map, very low-key
* smooth anchor scroll

Avoid:

* scroll-jacking
* parallax-heavy sections
* cards flying in from all directions
* animations required to understand content
* long animation delays

Respect:

```css
prefers-reduced-motion
```

This is one of those details that separates “looks cool” from “professionally built.”

# Mobile design

Mobile should be a first-class design, not a compressed desktop version.

Mobile priorities:

* hero headline readable in one screen
* no awkward horizontal card overflow
* metric cards stacked cleanly
* nav menu simple
* case study cards easy to tap
* no animation weirdness
* writing pages highly readable

For mobile, I’d use:

* single-column layout
* generous spacing
* sticky bottom/compact nav only if needed
* large tap targets
* shorter hero copy
